## About Me

Hello there! I'm Mario Daniel Panuco. I hold a Master's in Scientific
Computing and Applied Mathematics from UC Santa Cruz, which I came to by way
of a Computer Science undergraduate — algorithms and machine learning first,
then the numerical analysis and PDE theory that decide whether they work on
physical problems.

My focus is **scientific machine learning**: neural operators as PDE
surrogates, and the numerical questions that determine whether a learned
solver can be trusted — discretization error, stability, and how to measure
accuracy honestly on chaotic systems, where a single trajectory decorrelates
from the truth no matter how good the model is. Current work is a wavelet
diffusion neural operator on the Kuramoto–Sivashinsky equation, alongside
Monte Carlo methods\; computational fluid dynamics and computational genomics
are standing interests.

The [four-part series](@/posts/_index.md) on this site is the long-form
version of that thread: Euler's formula and Fourier series, the FFT and
spectral methods, wavelets and a minimal wavelet neural operator, and finally
a diffusion model that samples whole PDE trajectories in wavelet space — each
built in JAX and measured rather than asserted.

Elsewhere: [/projects](@/projects/_index.md) for smaller builds — a
graph-diffusion risk-scoring prototype, an HTTP server in C, neural-network
agents in Rust — and [/teaching](@/teaching.md) for my teaching experience.

**I'm looking for my next research position** — a PhD program, an industrial
research group, or a national laboratory. If you work on scientific machine
learning, numerical PDEs, or something adjacent, I'd be glad to talk.
